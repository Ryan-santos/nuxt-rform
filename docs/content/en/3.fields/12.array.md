`RArray`'s slot hands you `index`, and that is what the child uses as its
`name` — that is how the child's write lands in the right slot of the parent's
array.

## min and max

Below `min` the remove button disappears; at `max` the add button does. `min`
also seeds the initial rows.

## text.button

`text` is a **nested** object mirroring the component's `defaults.text` — never
a flattened prop such as `buttonText`. What you pass goes in raw, without a
prefix: a key of your app, or a `~~` literal.

## A note on performance

`RArray`'s `v-for` iterates `length` and passes each item through a row
component, instead of `v-for="(item, index) in model"`. That form read **every**
element in `RArray`'s own render, so one keystroke — a write to `array[i]` —
invalidated the whole list and repatched every sibling: 0.6 ms with 10 rows and
4.1 ms with 100, growing with the list where a flat form stayed flat.

Iterating `length` and passing `item` through a getter is not enough on its own:
`v-bind` on a `<slot>` normalizes the object and reads the getter all the same.
Only a component boundary scopes the dependency for real.
