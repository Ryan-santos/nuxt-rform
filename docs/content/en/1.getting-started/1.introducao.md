## The problem

A form has four responsibilities, and the temptation is to give each one its own
place: a component for the UI, a `ref` for the model, an `interface` for the
types and a schema for the validation. It works — until the fifth field, when
adding a phone number becomes four edits in four files, and the one you forget
only shows up in production.

## The decision

rform brings the four together **at the field**:

```vue
<RText
    name="cpf"
    label="CPF"
    mask="brCpf"
    rule="brCpf"
    required
/>
```

- `name` is the model key. There is no binding to write: the field reads and
  writes straight into the parent `RForm`'s model, through injection.
- `mask` is the format. It resolves by preset name and otherwise falls through
  to a raw maska pattern.
- `rule` is the validation. It takes a preset name, an object with args, a
  function, zod, or an array of any combination.
- `required` is the visual mark. It does not validate on its own — deliberately.

## What is left for you to decide

Two things, both from the outside in:

1. **Where the types and the validation come from** — that is what the
   [three `useRForm` modes](/en/form/form) choose.
2. **What the form looks like** — that is what
   [`ui` and `defineFieldDefaults`](/en/concepts/ui-e-defaults) decide, without
   any field having to know.

## Composition, not configuration

Each field is built from smaller pieces — `RUtilsLabel`, `RUtilsPlaceholder`,
`RUtilsError`, `RUtilsLength`, `RUtilsLoading` — that read the parent field's
props through `useUtil`. That is not an internal detail: it is the reason a util
of **yours**, in `app/rform/utils`, starts contributing props to every field
that includes it.

The same goes for fields: a `.vue` in `app/rform/fields` is a first-class field
— it enters `FieldType`, the `components-map` and `defineFieldDefaults`, and can
be the target of a preset's `available`. See
[Your own fields](/en/concepts/campos-proprios).
